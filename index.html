How the numbers are worked out

Energy-duration requirement
FCR-N is symmetric and has to sustain full response for 1 h in each direction, so it reserves that much energy at the top and the bottom of the pack. FCR-D only has to sustain 20 min (15 min alert plus 5 min) in its delivery direction.
Counter-reserve headroom (Normal State Energy Management)
A power reservation kept back for SoC restoration. It can't be sold as capacity, but the energy behind it can still be traded intraday. FCR-N reserves 34% of C in both directions (±1.34·C), and this stacks on delivery, so C_max = P / 1.34. FCR-D reserves 20% of C in the opposite direction as power only, with no energy behind it, so a single-direction bid is limited by the inverter alone (C_max = P). Bidding up and down at the same time adds the two reservations to 1.2·C per direction, giving C_max = P / 1.2.
SoC floor and ceiling
Set by the energy reservation, duration × C, divided by usable energy. FCR-N is two-sided, so the band is squeezed from both ends. FCR-D up needs charge to discharge, so it has a floor and a 100% ceiling; FCR-D down needs room to charge, so it has a ceiling and a 0% floor.
Power vs energy binding
Maximum sellable power is the lower of the inverter/headroom ceiling and the energy ceiling. A short pack hits the energy limit first: its SoC band pinches shut and sellable capacity falls below the power ceiling. Below the NEM band sits AEM, where the unit is declared unavailable.
Suggested starting SoC
Around 50% for symmetric provision (FCR-N, or FCR-D up + down), around 100% for FCR-D up, and around 0% for FCR-D down.

Model constants: FCR-N NEM 34%, FCR-D NEM 20%, FCR-D endurance ⅓ h per direction. Usable energy U = energy × window. Figures are indicative for scenario framing and don't guarantee a prequalification outcome; real usable energy also shifts with state of health over the life of the asset.
